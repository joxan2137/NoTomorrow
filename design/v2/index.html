UI redesign proposal · No Tomorrow v2

Keep the ember.
Lose the empty space.

The current app is tidy, but most screens are grey numbers in lists, whole screens sit empty until there's data, and the partner feature the app is built around lives in the fifth tab. This proposal keeps what already works: the ember-on-black look, the iOS 26 Liquid Glass tab bar, and screens as quiet as they are today. It gives each screen one clear focus, draws data where a shape says more than a number, and brings your bro onto the screens where it matters.

01 · Diagnosis

What's holding
the current UI back

Six problems, each visible in the shipped iOS 26 build. None of them is about taste. They're about where the eye lands and what the screen tells you to do next.

Progress · Bro

1Screens go empty

With little data, Progress is one row over a black void, and the Bro tab is a sign-in box. Nothing on the screen says what to do to fill it.

Train

2Everything shouts equally

Three identical white “Start workout” pills plus an outlined fourth. The one routine you're scheduled for today looks exactly like the ones you aren't.

Fuel

3Numbers without shapes

Macros are three grey hairlines, the kcal ring has no colour, and Progress has no chart on its home screen. You read numbers instead of seeing trends.

Today

4The bro is an afterthought

The reason the app exists, keeping each other honest, is a grey sentence on Today and the fifth tab. The week strip only shows you, never both of you.

Active workout

5The workout is a spreadsheet

No sense of progress through the session, a small state change when a set is done, and no hint of what to lift today beyond “previous”.

Welcome

6The brand stops at the door

The welcome screen is loud and confident. Past it, Big Shoulders only shows up for a couple of hero numbers and ember is used sparingly, so the app reads as generic dark iOS.

The “current” screenshots are the repo's own iOS 26 simulator captures (design/parity/ios), taken with test data, which explains values like 17 300 kcal. The critique is about layout and hierarchy, not the numbers. The two other directions in design/ (stock iOS blue, brutalist yellow) were already explored and set aside; this builds on the chosen one.

02 · The redesign

Six moves

No new brand, no new navigation, no extra chrome. The same five tabs, fonts and ember, with fewer and clearer things on each screen.

1

One focal point per screen

Each tab lifts one thing onto a card with a faint ember wash: the next session on Today, today's routine on Train, your lift on Progress, the week you share on Bro. Everything else stays on the plain surfaces the app already uses.

2

Shapes where they help

Progress gets a real chart: estimated 1RM with record markers and ranges, a sparkline per lift, a body-weight trend drawn through the raw weigh-ins, and a weekly muscle map from the muscle_model.json the app already ships. The other tabs stay lists.

3

Your bro, where it matters

Two avatars and a one-tap answer on the next-session card. A dot under each day for who trained. One quiet line in the workout when he's training too. The Bro tab opens on your shared week instead of a settings-style page.

4

Colour with a job

Ember means you, progress and records. Green means your partner and “confirmed”. Red only ever means “missed”. Protein, carbs and fat get their own hues so the kcal ring shows what the calories are made of. No other colour is added.

5

A calmer workout screen

A thin rail shows where you are. The next weight is suggested in one line (“82.5 kg today, you hit 8 · 8 · 7 at 80”) with Undo. Ticks are bigger, done sets are tinted, the rest timer sits at thumb height, and the other exercises collapse into a plain list.

6

Native motion, kept

The Liquid Glass tab bar works as it does in the shipped app, built to the numbers in docs/android-glass.md. The selection swells on touch, becomes a magnifying lens with a spectral rim while it slides or you drag it, then settles into the flat pill. Ticking a record set shows a short PR toast.

In code

A sketch of the SwiftUI side. Compose mirrors it in designsystem/.

Theme.swift: new tokens

enum Colors {
    // …existing tokens unchanged…

    // Macro hues. Ember stays "you + progress".
    static let protein   = Color(hex: 0x5EB8FF)
    static let carbs     = Color(hex: 0xF5C04A)
    static let fat       = Color(hex: 0xB18CFF)
}

Components.swift: the focal card

/// One per screen: the thing the user came to do.
struct FocalCard<Content: View>: View {
    @ViewBuilder var content: () -> Content
    private let shape = RoundedRectangle(
        cornerRadius: NT.Radius.card, style: .continuous)

    var body: some View {
        content()
            .padding(NT.Spacing.cardPadding)
            .frame(maxWidth: .infinity, alignment: .leading)
            .background {
                RadialGradient(
                    colors: [NT.Colors.ember.opacity(0.13), .clear],
                    center: .topTrailing,
                    startRadius: 0, endRadius: 320)
                .background(NT.Colors.surface)
            }
            .clipShape(shape)
            .overlay(shape.strokeBorder(
                NT.Colors.ember.opacity(0.12)))
    }
}
05 · Shipping it

Three phases,
each shippable

Ordered so every phase lands on its own and improves the app before the next one starts. Both apps move together, as they do today.

Phase1

Foundation

  • Macro hues and the FocalCard style
  • Macro-coloured ring, sparkline
  • Week strip dots for both of you
No backend changes
Phase2

Today, Train, Workout

  • Next-session card with both of you
  • Today's routine card; routine rows with play
  • Workout rail, weight suggestion, bigger ticks
  • PR toast when a record set is ticked
No backend changes. Uses data the app already stores.
Phase3

Fuel, Progress, Bro

  • Ring and protein hint on Fuel
  • e1RM chart, muscle map, lift sparklines, body trend
  • Bro week grid and one-tap heads-ups
One small backend addition: a “started workout” attendance event, so “Tomek is training too” can show live.

Prototype: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step. Source lives in the NoTomorrow repo under design/v2/. All sample data is invented.
